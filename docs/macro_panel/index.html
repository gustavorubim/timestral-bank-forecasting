Timestral experiments

Banks: 37 granular lines x 22-variable macro panel

Baseline arima, horizon 8 steps. Relative MASE: Timestral 0.965, best known-covariate variant timestral_macro_known 1.039.

Backtest report: macro_panel

Headline findings: does a CCAR-style macro panel help Timestral forecast granular bank lines?

Caveat: scenario-style test. The macro covariates are the realised future paths over each 8-quarter window (known-future, like a CCAR/DFAST scenario), so this measures how well each model uses a scenario, not real-time skill. Data: FDIC call-report sums for JPM, BAC, C and WFC (bank subsidiaries only, not full FR Y-9C), 37 lines, 38 quarterly origins from 2015Q1 to 2024Q2, one-shot 8-quarter forecasts. Full panel = 22 macro variables; compact panel = curve level and slope, unemployment, GDP growth, Baa spread and house prices. Timestral ran with its default symmetric averaging.

  • Macro helps rate- and credit-sensitive lines and hurts balance-sheet levels. Comparing full-macro Timestral with plain Timestral (<1 = macro helps):
  • Biggest gains: interest expense 0.58, interest income 0.72, allowance (ALLL) 0.74, provisions 0.78, noninterest-bearing deposits 0.81. Those are deposit-beta, rate and credit-cycle channels.
  • Biggest losses: subordinated debt 1.39, noninterest expense 1.35, real-estate loans 1.33, CRE loans 1.32, total loans 1.28. Noninterest income is 1.25.
  • By group: income 0.94 (helps), assets 1.12 and liabilities 1.10 (hurt).
  • Full vs compact. The full panel beats the compact one on income (0.87 vs 0.94 vs ARIMA). The gain comes from interest expense (0.49 vs 0.62) and interest income (0.65 vs 0.82), where the extra rates (prime, mortgage, fed funds, the curve) matter. On assets and liabilities the two panels are about equal, and both are worse than no covariates. So 22 covariates are not worse than 6. The problem is conditioning slow-moving, management-driven levels (loan books, debt issuance, expense programmes) on macro at all.
  • By horizon. Plain Timestral beats ARIMA at every horizon (0.91 at h=1 to 0.985 at h=8). With macro, the whole panel goes from 0.96 at h=1 to 1.07 at h=8 vs ARIMA. Against plain Timestral it is 1.06 to 1.09 at all horizons, with no horizon where macro helps overall. Diebold-Mariano at h=8: full-macro Timestral is significantly worse than ARIMA on 22% of series and better on 4%.
  • By bank (full macro vs plain Timestral): JPM 0.99, BAC 1.03, C 1.08, WFC 1.21. WFC's balance sheet, flat under its asset cap, plausibly responds least to macro.
  • Rate-regime origins (relative MASE vs ARIMA on income lines; plain → full macro):
  • 2021Q4 (before the hikes): 1.02 → 0.72. Knowing the rate path is decisive here, and compact gives 0.84.
  • 2015Q4 (liftoff): 0.90 → 0.87.
  • 2019Q2 (cuts) and 2020Q1 (zero lower bound): 1.01 → 1.25 and 0.86 → 0.94. At these origins macro hurts every group, liabilities most (1.34 and 1.40). The COVID deposit surge is the opposite of what the unemployment and GDP path would imply historically.
  • 2023Q2 (peak): 0.62 → 0.80. Lagged deposit repricing, as in the term-structure experiment.
  • Calibration. 80% interval coverage is 85% with macro (both panels), 81% for plain Timestral and for ARIMA, and 74% for ARIMAX.
  • ARIMAX with three known-future regressors is the worst variant (1.18). It helps only interest income and expense and the ALLL, and it is badly off on sub debt (2.52) and RE loans (1.65).

Bottom line: with a known macro scenario, use the scenario for interest income and expense, provisions, the allowance and deposit mix. Forecast balance-sheet volumes and noninterest lines without covariates.
